\section{Recordings}
% =====================================================
The voice is Will's, speaking one English sentence: ``\textit{Saad and Will are electrowizards.}''
Both recordings are uncompressed WAV, \SI{48}{\kilo\hertz}, 24-bit PCM (stored in 32-bit integers), mono, \SI{4}{\second} long (192\,000 samples), not clipped, at distances of \SI{5}{\centi\metre} and \SI{1}{\metre}.
The Nyquist frequency is \SI{24}{\kilo\hertz}, so the full spectrum up to \SI{20}{\kilo\hertz} is available.
The peak amplitudes are 0.0753 (\SI{-22.5}{\decibel FS}) at \SI{5}{\centi\metre} and 0.0060 (\SI{-44.5}{\decibel FS}) at \SI{1}{\metre}, so neither recording is clipped; the \SI{1}{\metre} recording is \SI{22}{\decibel} quieter at its peak.

\section{Task 1: Time and Frequency Analysis}
% =====================================================

\subsection{Time and frequency domain plots}
The samples are read with \texttt{wavfile.read} and normalised by dividing by $2^{31}$, the full scale of the 32-bit container holding the 24-bit data, giving amplitudes in $[-1,1)$.
The time axis is built with \texttt{linspace} from \SI{0}{\second} to $N/f_s$.
The spectrum is $X = \frac{1}{N}\,\mathrm{FFT}(x)$, with the $1/N$ scaling making the magnitude independent of recording length, and is shown as $20\log_{10}|X|$ in dBFS.
Only the first $N/2$ bins are plotted, from DC up to the Nyquist frequency, with bin spacing $f_s/N$, because the spectrum of a real signal is mirrored.
The frequency axis is logarithmic.

\subsection{Vowel fundamentals}
In both recordings the vowel energy appears as a series of peaks, the first at \SIrange{93}{117}{\hertz}, with the following peaks spaced at the same interval.
At \SI{5}{\centi\metre}, for example, the peaks in the /a/ of ``Saad'' lie at \SI{104}{}, \SI{208}{}, \SI{312}{} and \SI{416}{\hertz}; in the /e/ at \SI{95}{}, \SI{190}{}, \SI{285}{} and \SI{380}{\hertz}.
This equal spacing is the harmonic series of a voiced vowel, so the lowest peak of each series was taken as the fundamental $f_0$.
A fundamental of about \SI{100}{\hertz} is consistent with an adult male speaker.
The pitch drifts through the sentence, from \SI{104}{\hertz} to \SI{93}{\hertz} at \SI{5}{\centi\metre}, and starts higher in the \SI{1}{\metre} take (\SI{117}{\hertz}, falling to about \SI{100}{\hertz}), because the two recordings are separate takes.

\subsection{Consonant range}
Above about \SI{2}{\kilo\hertz} the harmonic series fades and a broad, noise-like hump appears with its peak at \SIrange{5}{6}{\kilo\hertz}.
The absence of harmonic structure and the broadband shape are characteristic of consonants such as /s/ and /z/ in the sentence.
At \SI{5}{\centi\metre} the hump reaches about \SI{-82}{\decibel FS} at \SI{6}{\kilo\hertz} and the level stays above the noise floor up to about \SI{12}{\kilo\hertz}, after which it falls steeply to the floor at about \SI{16}{\kilo\hertz}.
The consonant range is therefore marked from \SI{2}{\kilo\hertz} to \SI{12}{\kilo\hertz}.
At \SI{1}{\metre} the hump is lower in level (about \SI{-107}{\decibel FS} at \SI{5}{\kilo\hertz}) and drops into the noise floor sooner, reaching it at about \SI{14}{\kilo\hertz}, so the range is marked \SIrange{2}{10}{\kilo\hertz}.

\subsection{Noise region}
Above \SI{16}{\kilo\hertz} (\SI{14}{\kilo\hertz} at \SI{1}{\metre}) both spectra are flat at about \SI{-143}{\decibel FS} (power level smoothed over \SI{50}{\hertz}) with no harmonic structure.
The level is the same at both distances, so it does not come from the voice and is attributed to electronic noise of the recording chain.
The silent part at the end of each recording is consistent with this: it has an RMS level of \SI{-96.6}{\decibel FS} at \SI{1}{\metre}, flat across the spectrum at about \SI{-137}{\decibel FS} per bin, and \SI{-90.7}{\decibel FS} at \SI{5}{\centi\metre}, with the extra energy at low frequencies (about \SI{-122}{\decibel FS} per bin between \SIrange{60}{300}{\hertz} against \SI{-135}{\decibel FS} above \SI{5}{\kilo\hertz}).
The region \SIrange{16}{24}{\kilo\hertz} is marked as noise only for both recordings.

\section{Task 2: Speech Enhancement}
% =====================================================

\subsection{Method}
The recording is transformed with the FFT, every bin is multiplied by a gain array and the result is transformed back with the IFFT.
The gain array has the same length $N$ as the signal.
A band is defined by \texttt{gain\_ctrl(low, high, gain\_dB, ...)}: a trapezoidal profile in dB is built over the positive frequencies with \texttt{np.interp}, converted to linear gain with $10^{\mathrm{dB}/20}$, multiplied into the array, and mirrored onto the negative-frequency bins so that the IFFT output is real.
The parameter $q\in[0,1]$ sets the fraction of the band width over which each edge ramps ($q=0$ ramps over one bin, a rectangle).
Hard edges produced audible ringing (Gibbs phenomenon), so the band edges are interpolated, with $q=0.1$ for broad bands and $q=0.25$--$0.3$ for the narrower bands.
Bands that start at DC or end at Nyquist have their ramp placed outside the spectrum so the ends receive the full gain, which fixed a gain curve that rose at both ends of the spectrum.
Several bands can be stacked on one array by calling \texttt{gain\_ctrl} repeatedly.
After the IFFT only the real part is kept (\texttt{np.real}); the imaginary part is numerically close to zero because of the mirrored gain array.
An identity test, with a gain array of ones, confirmed that the output equals the input (\texttt{np.allclose}), so the pipeline itself introduces no change.
The result is scaled by $2^{31}-1$ and saved as 32-bit integer WAV, the same format as the input.

\subsection{Noise to be removed}
\textbf{\SI{5}{\centi\metre}:}
low-frequency rumble below about \SI{80}{\hertz}, which lies below the fundamental of the voice, and hiss above about \SI{12}{\kilo\hertz}, where no voice energy remains.
Above \SI{16}{\kilo\hertz} only the flat electronic floor of about \SI{-143}{\decibel FS} is present.
The spectrum between \SIrange{20}{100}{\hertz} is at \SIrange{-70}{-80}{\decibel FS}, as high as the voice harmonics themselves, whereas the \SI{1}{\metre} recording has only about \SI{-107}{\decibel FS} at \SI{50}{\hertz}.

\textbf{\SI{1}{\metre}:}
room rumble below about \SI{85}{\hertz}, a narrow spike near \SI{500}{\hertz} (\SIrange{480}{505}{\hertz}), possibly the 10th harmonic of mains hum, a room-resonance bump around \SIrange{2.2}{2.8}{\kilo\hertz} that is higher than in the \SI{5}{\centi\metre} recording, and hiss above \SI{13}{\kilo\hertz}, where the consonants have already ended.
The recording at \SI{1}{\metre} also contains room reverberation, which cannot be removed with a frequency-domain gain, so an audible difference to the \SI{5}{\centi\metre} recording remains.

\subsection{Bass loss with distance and pop sounds}
\textbf{\SI{5}{\centi\metre}:}
close to the microphone the low frequencies are already clear, so the bass boost is small, and the main risk is pop sounds.
A pop was identified between \SI{1.28}{\second} and \SI{1.30}{\second} (measured in a DAW), lasting about \SI{50}{\milli\second}.
In the \SIrange{1.28}{1.30}{\second} window the RMS level is \SI{-29.8}{\decibel FS}, \SI{15}{\decibel} above the \SI{-44.9}{\decibel FS} of the preceding \SI{80}{\milli\second}; the same window in the \SI{1}{\metre} recording shows no such rise (\SI{-60.5}{\decibel FS} against \SI{-61.7}{\decibel FS}).
Most of its energy lies below \SI{150}{\hertz}, but it extends throughout the spectrum and so cannot be removed completely with a gain curve.
A low cut from DC to \SI{80}{\hertz}, below the fundamental of about \SI{95}{\hertz}, removes rumble and the largest part of the pop energy, and a modest boost of \SI{+4}{\decibel} between \SI{95}{\hertz} and \SI{400}{\hertz}, covering the first harmonics, adds depth.
Brightness is added in the noisy consonant region (\SIrange{3}{10}{\kilo\hertz}) for clarity, and hiss is cut above \SI{12.2}{\kilo\hertz}.

\textbf{\SI{1}{\metre}:}
the \SI{1}{\metre} recording is about \SI{21}{\decibel} quieter than the \SI{5}{\centi\metre} recording at \SI{100}{\hertz} and \SI{17}{\decibel} quieter at \SI{1}{\kilo\hertz}, and \SI{35}{\decibel} quieter at \SI{50}{\hertz} (smoothed spectra).
Bass is lost with distance and the fundamental measured higher than at \SI{5}{\centi\metre} (Table~\ref{tab:vowels}), so a larger boost of \SI{+6}{\decibel} is applied between \SI{95}{\hertz} and \SI{180}{\hertz} to restore depth, with a low cut below \SI{85}{\hertz} to remove ambient rumble.
The overall level of the \SI{1}{\metre} recording is raised by \SI{21}{\decibel} so that its peaks match the \SI{5}{\centi\metre} recording (raw peaks \SI{-44.5}{\decibel FS} and \SI{-22.5}{\decibel FS}); the enhanced peaks are 0.122 and 0.147 of full scale.
This also lifts the noise floor, which is audible once the voice ends (RMS \SI{-73}{\decibel FS} in the enhanced \SI{1}{\metre} recording, from \SI{-96.6}{\decibel FS} before enhancement, against \SI{-94}{\decibel FS} for the enhanced \SI{5}{\centi\metre} recording), and is the reason for the strong high cut and the notch at \SI{500}{\hertz}.
The band-wise gains in Table~\ref{tab:gains} are applied on top of the \SI{21}{\decibel} level match.

\section{Task 3: Aural Exciter}
% =====================================================

\subsection{Design overview}
The exciter is built on the \SI{5}{\centi\metre} recording and follows the Aphex block diagram (Figure~\ref{fig:block}).
The dry signal passes straight through to the mixer.
In the exciter sidechain a band of the signal is isolated by zeroing all FFT bins outside it (\texttt{band\_pass}, built from two \texttt{gain\_ctrl} calls with $-\infty$\,dB, in place of a tunable high-pass filter), normalised to a peak of 1 so that the drive is always effective, and passed through $\tanh(\mathrm{drive}\cdot x)$.
A second band-pass then keeps only the wanted harmonics, which are scaled by the mix ratio and added to the dry signal.

$\tanh$ only starts to distort once its input reaches $\pm1$.
For a sine input of sufficient drive the peaks are squashed into a square-like shape, which adds sine waves at multiples of the input frequency.
Because $\tanh$ is odd, the top and bottom are squashed equally and only odd harmonics are produced.
A sidechain centred on \SI{2}{\kilo\hertz} therefore places the third harmonic at \SI{6}{\kilo\hertz}, where the ear is most sensitive, and the fifth at \SI{10}{\kilo\hertz}, which is not too high.
Only a band is fed to the non-linearity because the bandwidth of the output grows with the bandwidth of the input and a wide input produces muddy, unpleasant intermodulation.
Only some harmonics are kept so that the generated content stays in the region where it is wanted (above \SI{5.5}{\kilo\hertz}) and the low harmonics are not added to the voice.
The big-bottom sidechain of the Aphex diagram (low-pass \SIrange{50}{190}{\hertz}) is not implemented.

\subsection{Pre-processing}
The exciter is run on the raw \SI{5}{\centi\metre} recording.
The pop at \SIrange{1.28}{1.30}{\second} lies mostly below \SI{150}{\hertz} (\SI{15}{\decibel} above the surrounding signal in the raw recording), outside the sidechain band, so it is not fed into the non-linearity and no separate pop removal is applied in this task.

\subsection{Iterative development}
Each iteration was judged by listening and by plotting the dry voice, the full $\tanh$ output and the added harmonics on one spectrum (Figures~\ref{fig:iter} and~\ref{fig:ba}).
Parameters are given as sidechain band, harmonic selection band, drive and mix.
The dry recording has a peak of 0.0753 and an RMS of 0.0082.
For each iteration the level of the added harmonics relative to the dry voice in \SIrange{5.5}{7}{\kilo\hertz}, and the peak and RMS of the output, are given in Table~\ref{tab:iter}.

\subsubsection{Iteration 1: initial design}
Sidechain \SIrange{1750}{2250}{\hertz} (\SI{500}{\hertz} wide, centred on $f=\SI{2}{\kilo\hertz}$), harmonics kept \SIrange{5}{7}{\kilo\hertz}, drive 10, mix 0.5.
A band-pass was used instead of a high-pass because the narrower input reduces mud.
The plot shows that $\tanh$ works as expected: only odd harmonics appear, at \SI{6}{\kilo\hertz} (3rd), \SI{10}{\kilo\hertz} (5th) and \SI{14}{\kilo\hertz} (7th).
The result sounds robotic with a high-frequency metallic character.
The added harmonics are \SI{15.4}{\decibel} louder than the voice itself in \SIrange{5.5}{7}{\kilo\hertz}, and the output peak is 0.242, 3.2 times the dry peak, with the RMS tripled to 0.0252, so the drive and mix were reduced.

\subsubsection{Iteration 2: lower drive and mix}
Same bands, drive 3 and mix 0.1, so that less signal is pushed into $\tanh$ and the added harmonics are quieter.
The added harmonic peaks sit at about \SI{-90}{\decibel FS} around \SI{6}{\kilo\hertz}.
The harmonics are now \SI{12.5}{\decibel} below the voice and the output peak is unchanged (0.0753).
The boost in the desired region is clearly audible and the k and click sounds are more present, but the effect is limited to a single narrow region, so the next step widens the range of frequencies.

\subsubsection{Iteration 3: wider bands}
Sidechain \SIrange{2000}{4000}{\hertz}, harmonics kept \SIrange{4}{12}{\kilo\hertz}, drive 3, mix 0.25.
The harmonics now cover a wider range, but the result shows more ringing than iteration 1; the output peak rises to 0.129 (1.7 times the dry peak) although the RMS stays at 0.0084.

\subsubsection{Iteration 4: later harmonic band, higher drive}
The harmonic selection band was moved to start at \SI{5.5}{\kilo\hertz} (keeping the third harmonic) and end at \SI{12}{\kilo\hertz}, with mix reduced to 0.15 to keep the output peak close to the level of the dry signal, and the drive was increased in steps up to 6.
With drive 6 and mix 0.15 the harmonics are \SI{8.2}{\decibel} below the voice and the output peak is 0.0985.
The harmonics are clearly audible, but the consonants become too prominent and the voice develops a lisp.

\subsubsection{Iteration 5: final design}
The drive was reduced to 4, the harmonic selection band was narrowed to end at \SI{10}{\kilo\hertz}, and the mix was varied up and down until a sweet spot was found at 0.25.
The k and s sounds are more pronounced and the voice sounds closer, while still sounding similar to the original.
The harmonics are \SI{10.2}{\decibel} below the voice in \SIrange{5.5}{7}{\kilo\hertz}, and the output has a peak of 0.116 and an RMS of 0.0084.

\subsection{Final design and justification}
\begin{table}[H]
  \centering
  \caption{Final exciter parameters.}
  \label{tab:final}
  \begin{tabular}{ll}
    \toprule
    Parameter & Value \\
    \midrule
    Input recording                & \SI{5}{\centi\metre}, raw \\
    Sidechain band (Hz)            & 2000--4000 \\
    Sidechain normalisation        & peak = 1 \\
    Non-linearity / drive          & $\tanh$, drive = 4 \\
    Harmonic selection band (Hz)   & 5500--10000 \\
    Mix ratio                      & 0.25 \\
    Big-bottom band (Hz) (if used) & not used \\
    Output peak / RMS              & 0.116 / 0.0084 (dry: 0.0753 / 0.0082) \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
\end{table}
The selection band starts at \SI{5.5}{\kilo\hertz} so that the third harmonic of the sidechain band is kept, and ends at \SI{10}{\kilo\hertz}, which removes the highest content that caused the lisp at drive 6.
A drive of 4 and a mix of 0.25 were the settings at which the consonants are more pronounced and the voice sounds closer without sounding different or harsh.
The sweep in Figure~\ref{fig:drive} shows why: at drive 2 the harmonics are \SI{23.8}{\decibel} below the voice and the output peak (0.083) barely changes, at drive 6 they are only \SI{3.8}{\decibel} below the voice, the peak rises to 0.132 and the RMS to 0.0090, and this is the setting that produced the lisp.
The final design keeps the RMS within \SI{0.2}{\decibel} of the dry voice (0.0084 against 0.0082) and the peak \SI{3.7}{\decibel} higher, whereas iteration 1 raised the RMS by \SI{9.7}{\decibel} and the peak by \SI{10.1}{\decibel}.
The added harmonics are all odd and lie in \SIrange{5.5}{10}{\kilo\hertz}, so the voice is made more prominent at the most sensitive frequencies without a noticeable increase in level.

\section{Usage instructions}
% =====================================================
From a terminal, while in the project folder, run:
\begin{verbatim}
python3 run_tasks.py
\end{verbatim}
The scripts need \texttt{numpy}, \texttt{scipy} and \texttt{matplotlib}.
The plots of Tasks 1--3 are opened with \texttt{plt.show()}.
The files \texttt{enhanced\_5cm.wav}, \texttt{enhanced\_1m.wav} and \texttt{exciter\_5cm\_5.wav} are written to the working directory.
\texttt{run\_tasks.py} imports \texttt{task1\_plot.py}, \texttt{task2\_enhance.py} and \texttt{task3\_exciter.py}, which must be in the same folder.
