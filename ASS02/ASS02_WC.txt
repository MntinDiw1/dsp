--- Task 2 (25%) --- 
coefficient design. Build the frequency response you want, IFFT it to get the impulse response, truncate it and window it. The key question to settle early is how the number of coefficients follows from the frequency resolution you need.

    Removal Choices:
    - remove 0-0.5Hz baseline wander
    - remove 45Hz+ to make room for the transition band.

    How to calculate number of coefficients and why?
    - 
    Tradeoff: Increasing resolution (number of bins) doubles the delay (not good for realtime.)

    The number of coefficients, M, is calculated based on the minimum width of the frequency range arguments.
    The minimum width is divided by 2 so that each range alway has at least 2 bins to operate on.
    The number of coefficients is then calculated by dividing the sample rate by the bin size.
    M is made even so that the impulse response can be split into two equal halves when it is shifted.


--- Task 3 (25%) --- 
the FIRfilter class with dofilter(v), one scalar in and one scalar out. The efficiency trap is named in the brief: don’t shift the whole delay line every sample. A ring buffer avoids that.

--- Task 4 (20%) --- 
LMS, added to the same class. You’ll need a noise reference, and for 50 Hz the usual choice is a generated sine.

Decision:
“A single LMS filter with a combined reference (DC + 50 Hz sine) was tried first, but its DC term adapted too quickly and acted as a high-pass filter, visibly flattening the P and T waves. Two cancellers in series, a slow 1-tap DC canceller followed by a 10-tap 50 Hz canceller, remove the same interference without distorting the heartbeat.”
