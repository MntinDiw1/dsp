\section{Recordings}
% =====================================================
Will spoke one English sentence, ``\textit{Saad and Will are electrowizards}'', recorded at \SI{5}{\centi\metre} and \SI{1}{\metre} as uncompressed mono WAV files (\SI{48}{\kilo\hertz}, 24-bit).

\section{Task 1: Time and Frequency Analysis}
% =====================================================
The samples are normalised by $2^{31}$, the full scale of the 32-bit signed integers used to store the 24-bit audio.
The spectrum is $X = \frac{1}{N}\,\mathrm{FFT}(x)$, plotted as $20\log_{10}|X|$ in decibels relative to full scale (dBFS): the loudest level possible in a digital system is \SI{0}{\decibel FS}.
Only the positive half is shown, because the spectrum of a real signal is mirrored.

\paragraph{Fundamentals.}
In both recordings the vowels appear as series of evenly spaced peaks.
The lowest peak of each series was taken as the fundamental, because the peaks above it sit at integer multiples of it (e.g.\ \SI{95}{}, \SI{190}{}, \SI{285}{} and \SI{380}{\hertz} at \SI{5}{\centi\metre}), which identifies a harmonic series rather than noise.
The fundamentals lie between about \SI{94}{} and \SI{103}{\hertz} at \SI{5}{\centi\metre} and \SI{101}{} and \SI{112}{\hertz} at \SI{1}{\metre}, as expected for an adult male voice.
They vary between recordings because these are separate takes.
Peaks below about \SI{80}{\hertz} have no harmonic series and were not chosen; these are low-frequency rumble from pop energy and room noise.

\section{Task 2: Speech Enhancement}
% =====================================================

\paragraph{Method.}
Each recording is transformed with the FFT, then every frequency bin is multiplied by a gain array (an array of ones), and the result is transformed back with the IFFT.
\texttt{gain\_ctrl(low, high, dB, q)} adds one band to the array: a trapezoid over the selected frequencies, converted to linear gain with $10^{\mathrm{dB}/20}$ and mirrored onto the negative frequencies so that the IFFT output is real (\texttt{np.real} only removes rounding residue).
Hard band edges caused audible ringing (Gibbs phenomenon), so each edge ramps over a fraction $q$ of the band width, with the nominal edge at the middle of the ramp.
An identity test (gain array at default values) confirmed that the pipeline alone does not change the signal at all.
The outputs are scaled by $2^{31}-1$ and saved as 32-bit integer WAV.

\paragraph{5\,cm.}
A low cut of \SI{-16}{\decibel} below \SI{80}{\hertz} removes rumble and part of the pop energy (the pop at \SIrange{1.28}{1.30}{\second} lies mostly below \SI{150}{\hertz}).
It stops at \SI{80}{\hertz} to keep the $\approx$\SI{95}{\hertz} fundamental, so the pop is slightly reduced but not removed.
The low end is already strong at this proximity, so the bass boost is small: \SI{+4}{\decibel} between \SIrange{95}{400}{\hertz}; this range covers the first few harmonics for depth.
The consonant region (\SIrange{3}{10}{\kilo\hertz}) is boosted by \SI{+6}{\decibel} for clarity and `brightness'.
A high cut of \SI{-12}{\decibel} above \SI{12.2}{\kilo\hertz} reduces hiss.
It is kept moderate because consonant energy extends to \SI{14.5}{\kilo\hertz} at this distance.

\section{Task 3: Aural Exciter}
% =====================================================

\subsection{Design overview}
The exciter is built on the \SI{5}{\centi\metre} recording and follows the Aphex block diagram (Figure~\ref{fig:block}).
The dry signal passes straight through to the mixer.
In the exciter sidechain a band of the signal is isolated by zeroing all FFT bins outside it (\texttt{band\_pass}, built from two \texttt{gain\_ctrl} calls with $-\infty$\,dB, in place of a tunable high-pass filter), normalised to a peak of 1 so that the drive is always effective, and passed through $\tanh(\mathrm{drive}\cdot x)$.
A second band-pass then keeps only the wanted harmonics, which are scaled by the mix ratio and added to the dry signal.

$\tanh$ only starts to distort once its input reaches $\pm1$.
For a sine input of sufficient drive the peaks are squashed into a square-like shape, which adds sine waves at multiples of the input frequency.
Because $\tanh$ is odd, the top and bottom are squashed equally and only odd harmonics are produced.
A sidechain centred on \SI{2}{\kilo\hertz} therefore places the third harmonic at \SI{6}{\kilo\hertz}, where the ear is most sensitive, and the fifth at \SI{10}{\kilo\hertz}, which is not too high.
Only a band is fed to the non-linearity because the bandwidth of the output grows with the bandwidth of the input and a wide input produces muddy, unpleasant intermodulation.
Only some harmonics are kept so that the generated content stays in the region where it is wanted (above \SI{5.5}{\kilo\hertz}) and the low harmonics are not added to the voice.
The big-bottom sidechain of the Aphex diagram (low-pass \SIrange{50}{190}{\hertz}) is not implemented.

\subsection{Pre-processing}
The exciter is run on the raw \SI{5}{\centi\metre} recording.
The pop at \SIrange{1.28}{1.30}{\second} lies mostly below \SI{150}{\hertz} (\SI{15}{\decibel} above the surrounding signal in the raw recording), outside the sidechain band, so it is not fed into the non-linearity and no separate pop removal is applied in this task.

\subsection{Iterative development}
Each iteration was judged by listening and by plotting the dry voice, the full $\tanh$ output and the added harmonics on one spectrum (Figures~\ref{fig:iter} and~\ref{fig:ba}).
Parameters are given as sidechain band, harmonic selection band, drive and mix.
The dry recording has a peak of 0.0753 and an RMS of 0.0082.
For each iteration the level of the added harmonics relative to the dry voice in \SIrange{5.5}{7}{\kilo\hertz}, and the peak and RMS of the output, are given in Table~\ref{tab:iter}.

\subsubsection{Iteration 1: initial design}
Sidechain \SIrange{1750}{2250}{\hertz} (\SI{500}{\hertz} wide, centred on $f=\SI{2}{\kilo\hertz}$), harmonics kept \SIrange{5}{7}{\kilo\hertz}, drive 10, mix 0.5.
A band-pass was used instead of a high-pass because the narrower input reduces mud.
The plot shows that $\tanh$ works as expected: only odd harmonics appear, at \SI{6}{\kilo\hertz} (3rd), \SI{10}{\kilo\hertz} (5th) and \SI{14}{\kilo\hertz} (7th).
The result sounds robotic with a high-frequency metallic character.
The added harmonics are \SI{15.4}{\decibel} louder than the voice itself in \SIrange{5.5}{7}{\kilo\hertz}, and the output peak is 0.242, 3.2 times the dry peak, with the RMS tripled to 0.0252, so the drive and mix were reduced.

\subsubsection{Iteration 2: lower drive and mix}
Same bands, drive 3 and mix 0.1, so that less signal is pushed into $\tanh$ and the added harmonics are quieter.
The added harmonic peaks sit at about \SI{-90}{\decibel FS} around \SI{6}{\kilo\hertz}.
The harmonics are now \SI{12.5}{\decibel} below the voice and the output peak is unchanged (0.0753).
The boost in the desired region is clearly audible and the k and click sounds are more present, but the effect is limited to a single narrow region, so the next step widens the range of frequencies.

\subsubsection{Iteration 3: wider bands}
Sidechain \SIrange{2000}{4000}{\hertz}, harmonics kept \SIrange{4}{12}{\kilo\hertz}, drive 3, mix 0.25.
The harmonics now cover a wider range, but the result shows more ringing than iteration 1; the output peak rises to 0.129 (1.7 times the dry peak) although the RMS stays at 0.0084.

\subsubsection{Iteration 4: later harmonic band, higher drive}
The harmonic selection band was moved to start at \SI{5.5}{\kilo\hertz} (keeping the third harmonic) and end at \SI{12}{\kilo\hertz}, with mix reduced to 0.15 to keep the output peak close to the level of the dry signal, and the drive was increased in steps up to 6.
With drive 6 and mix 0.15 the harmonics are \SI{8.2}{\decibel} below the voice and the output peak is 0.0985.
The harmonics are clearly audible, but the consonants become too prominent and the voice develops a lisp.

\subsubsection{Iteration 5: final design}
The drive was reduced to 4, the harmonic selection band was narrowed to end at \SI{10}{\kilo\hertz}, and the mix was varied up and down until a sweet spot was found at 0.25.
The k and s sounds are more pronounced and the voice sounds closer, while still sounding similar to the original.
The harmonics are \SI{10.2}{\decibel} below the voice in \SIrange{5.5}{7}{\kilo\hertz}, and the output has a peak of 0.116 and an RMS of 0.0084.

\subsection{Final design and justification}
\begin{table}[H]
  \centering
  \caption{Final exciter parameters.}
  \label{tab:final}
  \begin{tabular}{ll}
    \toprule
    Parameter & Value \\
    \midrule
    Input recording                & \SI{5}{\centi\metre}, raw \\
    Sidechain band (Hz)            & 2000--4000 \\
    Sidechain normalisation        & peak = 1 \\
    Non-linearity / drive          & $\tanh$, drive = 4 \\
    Harmonic selection band (Hz)   & 5500--10000 \\
    Mix ratio                      & 0.25 \\
    Big-bottom band (Hz) (if used) & not used \\
    Output peak / RMS              & 0.116 / 0.0084 (dry: 0.0753 / 0.0082) \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
\end{table}
The selection band starts at \SI{5.5}{\kilo\hertz} so that the third harmonic of the sidechain band is kept, and ends at \SI{10}{\kilo\hertz}, which removes the highest content that caused the lisp at drive 6.
A drive of 4 and a mix of 0.25 were the settings at which the consonants are more pronounced and the voice sounds closer without sounding different or harsh.
The sweep in Figure~\ref{fig:drive} shows why: at drive 2 the harmonics are \SI{23.8}{\decibel} below the voice and the output peak (0.083) barely changes, at drive 6 they are only \SI{3.8}{\decibel} below the voice, the peak rises to 0.132 and the RMS to 0.0090, and this is the setting that produced the lisp.
The final design keeps the RMS within \SI{0.2}{\decibel} of the dry voice (0.0084 against 0.0082) and the peak \SI{3.7}{\decibel} higher, whereas iteration 1 raised the RMS by \SI{9.7}{\decibel} and the peak by \SI{10.1}{\decibel}.
The added harmonics are all odd and lie in \SIrange{5.5}{10}{\kilo\hertz}, so the voice is made more prominent at the most sensitive frequencies without a noticeable increase in level.
