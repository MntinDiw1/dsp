--- Task 1 (20%) --- 
plot and diagnose. Identify baseline wander, 50 Hz and its harmonics, and anything else in the spectrum. This decides what Task 2 removes.

    Plotted in time domain with full & zoomed views. Also plotted in frequency domain to identify present frequencies.
    SAMPLE RATE = 250Hz

    ANALYSIS: Saad ECG 2
    - 'P' peaks more visible on 2nd reading, so chose over first
    - Large peak at the lowest frequencies (below ≈0.5 Hz): baseline wander.
    - the whole trace drifts down from about 6.5 mV to 3.8 mV: again, baseline wander, not signal weakening; the R peaks keep the same height above the trace
    - 50 Hz mains hum
    - Huge spike at 0Hz
    - Smaller spikes at 1.5, 3, 4.5Hz: the heartbeat and its harmonics
    - Frequency between peaks is (1/0.66s) = 1.5Hz (Confirms the small peak and harmonics being caused by heart itself)

    ANALYSIS: Will ECG
    - why was saad chosen instead

    Some broadband muscle noise (EMG) may sit underneath, but it overlaps the ECG, so a filter can’t remove it without removing heartbeat too. That’s probably the point of Bernd’s Moodle post.

--- Task 2 (25%) --- 
coefficient design. Build the frequency response you want, IFFT it to get the impulse response, truncate it and window it. The key question to settle early is how the number of coefficients follows from the frequency resolution you need.

    Removal Choices:
    - remove 0-0.5Hz baseline wander
    - remove 45Hz+ to make room for the transition band.

--- Task 3 (25%) --- 
the FIRfilter class with dofilter(v), one scalar in and one scalar out. The efficiency trap is named in the brief: don’t shift the whole delay line every sample. A ring buffer avoids that.

--- Task 4 (20%) --- 
LMS, added to the same class. You’ll need a noise reference, and for 50 Hz the usual choice is a generated sine.

--- Task 5 (20%) --- 
the matched filter for R-peak detection. Think about how a matched filter could be built from your own FIR class, because correl/conv are banned.

The tasks form a chain!
